1. Install FreeCAD without getting stuck

Download FreeCAD 1.1 or newer from the official FreeCAD site. Use the stable release, not a weekly build, unless you are intentionally testing. On Windows, the installer puts FreeCAD in Program Files and creates the user folder on first launch.

Start FreeCAD once before installing any addon. This creates the versioned user directory that holds the Mod folder. If you skip this step, an addon installer may not find the right place.

Version matters. FreeCAD 1.0 and 1.1 differ in their user folder layout. This guide uses 1.1. If a menu or path does not match, check your version before assuming the tutorial is wrong.

For this guide you only need the PartDesign and Sketcher workbenches. Ignore the other workbenches for now. You are not missing anything; they are different tools for different jobs.

2. The five concepts that cover 90% of CAD

CAD looks enormous because every tool has a button. The underlying model is small. Learn these five ideas and the rest becomes vocabulary.

ConceptWhat it meansWhy it matters
SketchA 2D profile on a plane or face.It is the DNA of the feature. A bad sketch makes every later feature fragile.
ConstraintA rule that removes freedom: coincident, horizontal, vertical, distance, radius.Constraints express design intent. Dimensions drive the model; constraints keep it from drifting.
Pad / PocketAdd or remove material from a sketch along a direction.This is how a 2D profile becomes a 3D feature.
BodyA container for one connected part and its feature tree.One part per body keeps the tree understandable and the model editable.
ExportSTL for printing, STEP for CAD interchange.A model that cannot leave the app is not finished.

Two more terms will come up constantly: degrees of freedom (DoF) and closed profile. DoF is how much the sketch can still move. Zero is the goal. A closed profile is a wire with no gaps; Pad and Pocket require one.

3. Your first sketch, constrained

This is the part most tutorials rush. Do it slowly once and the rest of CAD gets easier.

  1. Create a new document (Ctrl + N).
  2. Switch to PartDesign. Create a Body.
  3. Create a Sketch and attach it to the XY plane.
  4. Draw a rectangle with the rectangle tool. Do not worry about exact size yet.
  5. Add Coincident constraints at the four corners if the rectangle tool did not already join them.
  6. Add Horizontal and Vertical constraints to the lines. A rectangle should have two of each.
  7. Add a Distance constraint for width and one for height.
  8. Anchor one corner to the origin with a Coincident constraint between the corner and the origin point.

The sketch is ready when FreeCAD says it is fully constrained and the number of degrees of freedom is zero. If it still moves, do not pad it. Find the missing constraint first.

The senior-engineer rule: a sketch you cannot fully explain is a sketch you cannot safely edit. If you do not know why a constraint is there, delete it and re-add the one that expresses the design intent.

4. From sketch to solid: Pad, Pocket, Hole

Close Sketcher. Select the sketch in the tree and press Pad. Set a length that makes sense for the part, not a random number. Confirm. You now have a solid.

For a bracket, the next features are functional: mounting holes and a lightening pocket. Add those before cosmetic fillets. For a hole, create a sketch on the face where the hole belongs, draw a circle, constrain its center and diameter, close the sketch, and use Pocket or the Hole feature.

  • M3 clearance: 3.2 to 3.4 mm. For a printed self-tapping hole, 2.5 mm is a starting point.
  • Edge distance: keep at least one hole diameter of material around the hole.
  • Pocket depth: leave enough material for the load path. A pocket is not free strength.

5. Fillets, chamfers, and printability

Fillets are finishing features. Add them after the functional geometry works. For FDM printing, 0.5 to 2 mm on external corners is usually enough. Do not fillet faces that must sit flush, and do not use a radius larger than the local edge can support.

Check the basics before exporting: the body is a valid, closed solid; walls are at least 1.2 mm for FDM; holes are at least 2 mm unless you plan to drill them; sharp internal corners are either intentional or relieved.

6. Export STL and STEP

Select the body, then export. Use STL for printing and STEP for CAD interchange. A valid solid should export without warnings. If the exporter complains, go back to the first failing feature instead of trying a different format.

Open the STL in a slicer or viewer. Check the scale and the orientation. A part that is watertight but 10 times too large is still wrong.

8. Common errors and exactly how to fix them

The sketch will not close

Zoom in on the endpoints. Add Coincident constraints between the two points. A profile can look closed at normal zoom and still have a 0.1 mm gap. Use the sketch solver messages and the "open wires" count, not your eyes.

Pad fails

The profile is usually open, or it contains zero-length geometry, or the sketch is not attached to a valid plane. Fix the sketch first. Do not keep pressing Pad and hoping.

The sketch has too many degrees of freedom

Add coincident constraints first, then horizontal and vertical, then dimensions. Anchor the sketch to the origin. If a dimension repeats something the geometry already enforces, remove it; redundant constraints fight the solver.

The body is invalid

Read the first red feature, not the last. A bad fillet can make every later feature look broken. Reduce the radius, remove the feature, or rebuild the profile. Use Part Check Geometry if the cause is not obvious.

The fillet fails

The radius is usually larger than the local edge can support, or the edge is shared with a tangent face. Try a smaller radius, apply the fillet earlier, or chamfer instead.

9. How to practice so it sticks

  • One part per session. Finish it. A finished simple part teaches more than three abandoned complex ones.
  • Change one dimension after every part. If the model breaks, you found the weak constraint.
  • Rebuild from memory. Close the tutorial and make the same part again. This is the step that converts watching into skill.
  • Review before you print. Closed solid, wall thickness, hole size, and orientation. A model is not done when it looks done.
  • Keep a mistake log. Three recurring errors are more useful to fix than twenty new tools.

FAQ

How long does it take to learn FreeCAD?

A simple printable part is realistic in 10 to 20 focused hours. Comfort with parametric edits, assemblies, and manufacturability usually takes three to six months of deliberate practice.

Is FreeCAD harder than Fusion 360?

FreeCAD exposes more of the machinery, so the first hour is less guided. The core workflow is the same: sketch, constrain, pad, pocket, fillet, export. Once the concepts click, the difficulty is comparable.

Why does my FreeCAD pad fail?

The most common cause is an open profile. A pad needs a closed wire. Check the endpoints, add Coincident constraints, and confirm the sketch has no open wires before padding.

Do I need to fully constrain every sketch?

Yes, for work you intend to edit. A fully constrained sketch has zero degrees of freedom. If the sketch can still move, later dimension changes will move geometry you did not intend to change.

What should I model first in FreeCAD?

A phone stand or a simple bracket. Both use a sketch, a pad, a pocket or hole, and a fillet, and both produce a part you can actually hold or print.
